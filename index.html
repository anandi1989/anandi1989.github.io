About

I have spent the last 14 years trying to understand how machine learning systems behave when brought face-to-face with real-world complexity. My background started in chemical engineering and mathematical modeling at Pune University, where I first developed an appreciation for stochastic optimization and probabilistic reasoning.

Today, I lead an applied AI science team at Verizon, working alongside engineers and researchers on problems in customer personalization, agentic shopping architectures, and real-time ranking models. Prior to this, I spent several years at PwC Labs and Accenture Research, exploring document intelligence, multi-agent frameworks, and probabilistic sequence models—one of which turned into a humble US patent for hybrid AI processing.

I find financial data challenges and prediction markets to be clean grounds for testing ML hypotheses because markets relentlessly penalize overfitting. Exploring spaces like decentralized quantitative modeling competitions and event prediction platforms alongside agentic systems keeps me curious about signal design, meta-learning, and non-stationary distribution shifts.

I am also a strong believer in open research, FOSS, and community building. As an ambassador for the global Quant Club initiative and an active participant in tech upskilling efforts, I enjoy sharing what I learn along the way and learning from the broader engineering community.
